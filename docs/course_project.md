## 1. Introduction

March Madness is a spectacle that captures everyone from die-hard college sports fans and casual bracket-fillers. With its shocking upsets and unexpected outcomes, March Madness embodies its name. In 2025 alone, over 24 million brackets were submitted to ESPN, representing over one billion picks made (ESPN, 2025). Many wonder what characteristics in a team lead to resounding March Madness success. What makes successful March Madness teams? Specifically, can we predict the likelihood of a team making it deep into the playoffs based on their offensive and defensive performance? Answers to these questions hold the potential to help teams, coaches, and fans alike. 

## 2. Methods

In order to analyze these questions, a large amount of data was needed. A dataset from Kaggle that compiled historical data from 2002 to 2025 on NCAA Division 1 college men's basketball teams was used to do this analysis. The Kaggle dataset sourced its data from Kenpom advanced metrics which includes thousands of teams from each year. In preparation for analyzing the data, much data cleaning was needed. Focusing on two specific characteristics of each team was key. The most important characteristics focused on during this study were adjusted offensive efficiency and adjusted defensive efficiency. The reason for choosing these two specific characteristics was that they are the crux of all teams. It shows how well a team can defend against an opponent and how well a team can score against them. Calculating offensive efficiency consists of (points scored/possessions) \* 100 and for defensive efficiency (points allowed/possessions) \* 100\. This gives a raw efficiency; however, the metrics used were adjusted efficiencies to understand each team better. Adjusted offensive efficiency is the raw offensive efficiency adjusted for the strength of the defense they face. Similarly, adjusted defensive efficiency is the raw defensive efficiency adjusted by the strength of the offense they face. Adjusting these metrics is important because it better explains their true offensive and defensive efficiency. These calculations were made by Kenpom. Focusing on these two characteristics made analyzing the extensive data set much easier. The next major part of cleaning the data was removing teams not in the tournament since the data set includes every NCAA Division 1 men's college basketball team. In order to do this, a list was compiled consisting of every team in the March Madness tournament for each year spanning from 2003 to 2024\. Then, using the list to remove any team that was not in the list of teams for their respective year. Similarly, another data frame contained sets of the Elite Eight teams, the Final Four teams, and finally, the champions.   
The first method for analyzing the data was K-means clustering, an unsupervised machine-learning algorithm used to cluster different points in the data together. The goal for using K-means was to separate teams into three distinct groups based only on offensive and defensive efficiency. By visualizing these clusters and overlaying tournament results (Elite Eight, Final Four, Champion) aimed to identify whether one of the clusters consistently contained the most successful teams. The K-Means model did not use tournament outcomes to create clusters, only the offensive and defensive efficiency of the data frame containing tournament teams. This allowed for certain statistical profiles that correlate to deeper tournament runs naturally.  
The second method used was logistic regression, a supervised learning algorithm for binary classification. Using three different logistic regression models, one to predict whether a team would make it to the Elite Eight, one for the Final Four, and one for winning the championship. Each model contains two features, offensive and defensive efficiency, to predict the probability of success. For example, the Final Four model outputs a probability of 0 to 1, indicating how likely a team is to reach the Final Four based on their efficiencies. The logistic regression is used to determine whether a team will make it to the Elite Eight, Final Four, or win the tournament based on their defensive and offensive efficiency. 

## 3. Results

The K-means model separated the NCAA tournament teams into three clusters based on offensive and defensive efficiency. As shown in Figure 1, each cluster showed clear profiles: 

**Figure 1**: K-Means clustering of NCAA Division 1 men's basketball teams according to teams adjusted defensive and offensive efficiency.

Cluster 0 (red) showed low offensive and defensive efficiency, Cluster 1 (blue) showed moderate offensive efficiency and above-average defensive efficiency, and Cluster 2 (green) showed high offensive efficiency and strong defensive efficiency. Elite Eight, Final Four, and championship teams were overlaid to analyze the relationship between clusters and tournament success, as shown in Figure 2\. Visually, a large majority of successful teams fell inside of cluster 2\.   
**Figure 2**: K-Means clustering of NCAA Division 1 men's basketball teams according to teams adjusted defensive and offensive efficiency. Overlaid with Elite Eight, Final Four, and Championship teams

This suggested a strong correlation between the efficiency profile and tournament placement. The quantified breakdown showed that 40% of the Elite Eight teams were in Cluster 1 and 60% in Cluster 2\. For the Final Four, 39.8% fell into Cluster 1, and 60.2% fell into Cluster 2\. Finally, for champions, 38.1% of teams fell into Cluster 1 and 61.9% in Cluster 2\. These results show that over 60% of champions and the Final Four teams belong to a single efficiency cluster, and 100% of successful teams did not fall into cluster 0\. Thus, certain defensive and offensive efficiencies are more conducive to success.   
The three logistic regressions were trained to predict whether a team would reach the Elite Eight, Final Four, or be a champion using their offensive and defensive efficiency. The coefficient represents the change in the log odds of making it further in the tournament, resulting in a one-unit change in offensive and defensive efficiency. The Elite Eight coefficient for offensive efficiency was \+0.2981 and \-0.3063 for defensive. The Final Four was \+0.299 for offensive efficiency and \-0.3166 for defensive. Champion was \+0.4557 for offensive efficiency and \-0.4046 for defensive. Higher offensive efficiency increases the likelihood of tournament success, and lower defensive efficiency also increases the likelihood of tournament success. 

## 4. Discussion

The study clearly distinguishes successful March Madness teams from other tournament teams based on offensive and defensive efficiency. The K-Means clustering model with Elite Eight, Final Four, and championship teams overlaid provided strong evidence that these two metrics play a critical role in determining tournament success. The most obvious conclusion that could be drawn from the clustering is that teams assigned to Cluster 0 (low offense and defense efficiency) have never advanced to the Elite Eight over the past 20 years. Thus, the clusters demonstrated meaningful patterns that address the core question: Are there identifiable statistical traits that predict deep tournament success? This suggests that teams with below-average efficiencies will unlikely make a deep run in the tournament. The analysis also showed how roughly 60% of Elite Eight, Final Four, and champion teams belong to Cluster 2\. Cluster 2 represents teams with strong offensive and strong defensive efficiency. This highlights that while offensive efficiency will give you a better chance of being successful overall, in order to take the next step, a team needs to focus on defensive efficiency as well. The data suggests that offensive strength might help teams survive early rounds but defensive efficiency is what separates contenders from champions. This study offers a beneficial filtering mechanism for fans and analysts building predictive models or filling out brackets.   
While K-Means clustering analysis showed meaningful results, there were plenty of limitations and room for improvement. The K-Means model assumes that clusters are spherical and that they may not account for tournament outcome labels when making clusters. This means that clusters are based purely on feature similarity, not outcome optimization.   
Given the analysis's goals, K-Means clustering is highly effective as a first-pass diagnostic tool. It is simple, interpretable, and effective at separating teams using offensive and defensive efficiency. Although it is not predictive, it provides important insights for analysts and fans.   
The logistic regression model offers a interpretable understanding of the probability of March Madness success based on offensive and defensive efficiency. Unlike K-Means which is unsupervised logistic regression incorporated tournament outcomes. This makes it well suited for predictive tasks.   
The three logistic regression models were trained to estimate the probability that a team would reach the Elite Eight, Final Four, or win the tournament. The coefficient analysis revealed consistent trends that high offensive efficiency increased the likelihood of tournament success, and lower defensive efficiency also increased the odds of success, as seen in the K-Means model. The logistic regression model for predicting champions assigned the highest weight to these factors, reinforcing the notion that championship teams must excel on both ends of the court.   
Logistic regression models carry several limitations. First, the binary nature ignores more tournament dynamics like matchups and seeding. In addition, the model assumes linear relations between efficiency metrics and log odds of success. This may overlook more complex patterns in the data. The logistic model showed high accuracy in predicting teams that do not advance to the tournament. However, when it came to identifying actual successful teams, the model struggled. This was mainly due to the lack of teams that made it to the Elite Eight, Final Four, and were champions in the past 20 years. 

## 5. Conclusion

This study showed that offensive and defensive efficiency are key predictors of March Madness success. K-Means clustering revealed that over 60% of Elite Eight, Final Four, and championship teams fell into a high efficiency cluster while no low efficiency teams made deep runs. Logistic regression supports this analysis showing that strong offensive and defensive metrics increase a team's chances. While K-means and logistic limitations held this analysis from giving perfect predictors these models help identify potential contenders and help inform bracket predictions.
